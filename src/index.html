About

I am a recent Computer Science and Engineering graduate from the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET). My research journey began with my undergraduate thesis on hardware prefetching, where I designed a neural-network prefetch filter that needs far less storage than the perceptron filter it replaces, at almost no cost in performance. I did this work under the guidance of Dr. Rifat Shahriyar and Dr. Abdullah Muzahid.

That work showed me how much a computer's performance depends on the parts that move data: what the processor fetches ahead of time, and how a storage engine lays out and reorganizes data on disk. Since then my interests have centered on computer architecture and database storage systems, especially LSM-tree key-value stores, where small policy decisions add up to large differences in speed and resource use. I'm most interested in the places where machine learning genuinely improves how these systems behave, which is what I'm exploring now at the CHiLL Lab through learned compaction for RocksDB. In the long run, I want this line of work to help make data centers far more energy-efficient.

Alongside research, I have spent about a year and a half as a software engineer, building backend services and LLM-based pipelines at companies in Dhaka and, remotely, in Toronto. That work taught me to find and fix real performance bottlenecks, such as cutting an API's p95 latency from 1851 ms to 30 ms, and to build software that other people rely on.

When I'm not reading papers or writing code, I enjoy traveling to different regions of Bangladesh and seeing how people there live. These trips show me everyday problems that rarely make it onto television or into newspapers. I believe this kind of firsthand understanding is what helps people from different countries understand one another.

I'm always happy to discuss computer architecture, LSM-tree-based storage engines and machine learning for systems, and I'm open to collaborations in these areas.

Research experience

Jan 2026 – Present

Remote

Graduate Research Intern @ CHiLL Lab, University of Massachusetts Boston

Computing Hardware & Large Data Lab · Advisor: Dr. Tarikul Islam Papon

  • Designing a learned compaction trigger for RocksDB-based LSM-tree key-value stores: a reinforcement-learning controller decides, level by level, whether to compact early or defer by adjusting the L0 file-count trigger and per-level size targets, while RocksDB keeps full control of file selection and correctness.
  • Uses an analytic LSM cost model as a prior with a learned residual on top, so the policy starts cold and adapts online to workload shifts without offline training or tuning sweeps; the goal is lower read and write amplification than stock RocksDB.
  • Implementing the controller inside RocksDB in C++, with training in Python; evaluating with db_bench on bare-metal Chameleon Cloud nodes using the Facebook production workload model of Cao et al. (FAST 2020).
