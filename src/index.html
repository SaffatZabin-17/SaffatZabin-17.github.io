About Me

I'm a computer science researcher from Dhaka, Bangladesh, with a B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from Bangladesh University of Engineering & Technology (BUET) (2020–2025, CGPA 3.59/4.00). I am currently applying to PhD programs in computer science.

My research sits where learned methods meet systems. For my undergraduate thesis I reproduced and then replaced the perceptron-based prefetch filter of Bhatia et al. (ISCA 2019) with a feed-forward neural filter that cut model weight storage by 93% and total filter memory overhead by 47% at under 1% IPC loss. I am now extending that instinct to storage: a physics-informed reinforcement learning compaction trigger for LSM-tree key-value stores that adapts online to workload shifts.

I also work on vision–language modelling for low-resource languages, where the scarcity of paired data makes efficiency a research constraint rather than an engineering detail, and on human-computer interaction—my CHI work studied how Bangladeshi citizens adapted during the 2024 nationwide internet shutdown.

Two years of industry engineering sharpened how I build and measure systems, and I bring that same rigour to research code. Outside the lab I read and travel—I hope to one day visit all 64 districts of Bangladesh (currently 14 and counting).

Research Interests

Database & Storage Systems

Machine Learning for Systems

Vision–Language & Low-Resource NLP

Human-Computer Interaction

Qualitative and mixed-methods study of how people adapt to infrastructure failure—internet shutdowns, degraded connectivity—and what that implies for resilient system design in the Global South.

Beyond the Blackout: Qualitative Insights and Sentiment Analysis on the Impact, Adaptation, and Design Solutions During the Internet Shutdown in Bangladesh

Farhan Tahmidul Karim*, Sardar Md. Saffat Zabin*, Ishika Tarin, Novia Nurain, A. B. M. Alim Al Islam

* Equal contribution (co-first authors)

The recent period of Internet restriction in Bangladesh, which occurred from July 14 to August 5 during the anti-discrimination student movement, had a significant impact on individuals' lives. This study aims to know how their communication, information access, and daily activities were affected and how they used to cope with that, again what were their expectations from social media and communication tools. Through interviews with 15 individuals from diverse demographics, supported by qualitative analysis and sentiment analysis, the findings provide valuable insights into the challenges faced, experiences shared, and recommendations offered by participants. Based on these findings, we propose design implications to develop a robust digital infrastructure capable of addressing similar challenges in future scenarios.

Align Where the Words Look: Cross-Attention-Guided Patch Alignment with Contrastive and Transport Regularization for Bengali Captioning

Riad Ahmed Anonto, Sardar Md. Saffat Zabin, M. Saifur Rahman

Vision–language systems for low-resource languages often produce fluent text about the wrong objects, because limited paired data and English-centric pretraining leave the visual grounding weak. We pair a frozen MaxViT encoder with a Bengali-native mBART-50 decoder through a lightweight bridge, and regularize alignment with three complementary objectives: a Patch-Alignment Loss that uses decoder cross-attention to match real and synthetic patch descriptors, InfoNCE to separate real from synthetic representations globally, and Sinkhorn-based optimal transport to keep fine-grained patch correspondence balanced. The approach reaches BLEU-4 of 12.29 on Flickr30k-1k and 12.00 on MSCOCO-1k while narrowing the real–synthetic centroid gap by 41%.
